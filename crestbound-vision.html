Why this isn't just another card game

We changed what a card is.

Every TCG (paper or digital) works the same way: a design team finishes a set, an art team illustrates it, and you open what they made. The set is a catalog. Completion is a checklist. Crestbound breaks that assumption entirely:

Everywhere else: the finished world

Sets designed years ahead · art commissioned per card · every player opens identical, pre-existing cards · story written in a boardroom · your name appears nowhere.

Crestbound: the found world

Cards exist sealed and unwritten until a player pulls them into being · the finder's name is printed on them forever, fleet-wide · the story is written by last season's battles · the world literally does not exist until the community finds it.

“Long before Aldermarch knew quiet… now the Crests are warming again, and somewhere a hand moves among them.” The founding lore. The hand is the player. That was always the design.

“AI-generated cards”: won't they be broken or ugly?

Creativity is generated. Fairness is engineered.

Nothing here is “let the AI improvise and hope.” Every generated Crest passes through a constitution, hard rules the game itself enforces before a card can exist:

Balance is a compiler, not a hope

The Design Law restricts every card to a fixed vocabulary of tribes, keywords, triggers and scaling ops the engine actually executes. The island lint rejects any card that plugs into no shared axis; the colour-pie lint rejects off-faction verbs. A card that can't compile against the engine cannot be born.

Quality has a gatekeeper

Every image already runs a vision-model QA loop before it's locked in fleet-wide: it redraws until brand rules pass (live today, iterating up to 10× per card). Stage 2 extends the same gate to card text. Operators keep one-click reroll & regen tooling, so nothing is irreversible.

No prompt-injection surface

Players never type anything. Discovery is a pull; all prompts are server-side templates. There is no path from a player's keyboard into the generator.

Your collection is forever

Every asset generates once ever, then lives permanently in the game: art, text and sound. Nothing you find or own can vanish, change under you, or be quietly rebalanced away.

Power creep is structurally capped

Generated cards draw from the same stat/cost budgets and rarity targets as the hand-tuned set: the Law fixes the shape of the set (enabler/payoff ratios, vanilla caps, mythic rules) so a lucky pull is a famous card, never a broken one.

Fairness is doctrine

One rule everywhere, enforced in review: nothing generated may advantage a specific player or server. Discovery grants fame (a name on a card), never power. (More on this doctrine two slides down.)

For the technically curious

Web-native by design. Secure by architecture. all in production

Crestbound is not a client you install: it's a browser game, playable in seconds on any phone or desktop. That choice is also what keeps it fair for everyone:

Zero-install, instant distribution

Pure web (HTTPS, single-page portal): no launcher, no app-store gatekeeper, no patch day. Every release this week reached 100% of players on their next page load. Fully responsive: the screenshots in this deck are a phone.

Cheating is structurally impossible

Every pack roll, XP spend, battle action and quest tick is resolved on the server; your browser only draws the pictures. A tampered client can change its own pixels; it cannot mint a card, fake a win, or spend a balance it doesn't have. Your opponents are playing the same honest game you are.

Identity without passwords

Sign-in is Discord OAuth: no credentials stored, sessions are bearer-token scoped, and a player only ever sees their own wallet and collection. Admin/operator surfaces are separately token-gated and never exposed to players.

Generation keys never touch the browser

All AI generation runs server-side behind our API: the provider key lives only on the server, prompts are code-owned templates, outputs pass the QA gate, and finished assets are served from our own database with immutable caching: the client consumes art; it can't commission it.

Abuse-hardened economy

Currency mutations are atomic, race-safe transactions (concurrent discovery is reservation-locked); spend endpoints re-validate balances server-side; generation is queued, paced, rate-limit-aware and wallet-guarded in code, proven this week in production.

Updates land while you sleep

New features reach every player on their next page load, no launcher, no patch day (seven releases shipped in a single day building what you're reading about). Data collection is minimal and governed by a published privacy policy.

Provenance without the blockchain nonsense

Cards that remember next

In every other game, your thousandth win with a card looks identical to your first. In Crestbound, each copy keeps its own chronicle, and shows it.

How it works (it's small)

Each owned copy carries per-copy counters (duels fought, victories, mythics slain, seasons survived) incremented by the battle engine we already run. A few integers per card. That's the whole cost.

What players see

Visual patina tiers: a veteran card's frame weathers, then gilds. Earned epithets appear on the card: “Oathsworn Paladin, the Unbroken: 214 victories.” Purely cosmetic, rendered as overlay layers on the existing card chrome.

Why it matters

The moment a duplicate has a biography, every card becomes sentimental and every collection becomes a memoir. And if trading ever ships, the history travels with the card: imagine being handed the exact Paladin that survived three hundred duels.

Zero balance impact: the chronicle never touches stats. It composes with The Discovery beautifully: the most coveted object in the game becomes a famous card with a famous history, found by a named player.

The platform's history becomes the game's canon

Event-born Crests: commemorated, never privileged next

Server Assistant stops real raids and protects real communities. Those moments can mint Crests: a genuine milestone on the platform seals a new commemorative Crest into the global pool: “The Siege Repelled,” art and flavour born from the shape of the event itself.

The Fairness Doctrine: one rule, applied everywhere

No feature may ever preferentiate a guild or a member. It is a design law with the same standing as the colour pie:

  • Event Crests enter the global pool for everyone, simultaneously: same odds, same quests, same price. The event server gets no exclusive copy, no head start, no discount.
  • The commemoration lives in lore, not access: the flavour text remembers the event; the card itself is standard-power, standard-rarity, findable by any player anywhere.
  • Discovery grants a name, never an edge: a finder's credit is fame; the card plays identically for all.
  • Chronicle & cosmetics never touch stats: everything earnable is visible, nothing earnable is power.

Why this matters to you: your wins are earned. No whale, no mega-server, no early adopter ever holds power you can't reach just by playing.

The meta writes the story, on film

The Living Chronicle next

MTG's story is written two years in advance by a committee. Crestbound's is written by last month's ladder. Every season closes with the faction standings decided by real duels, and the winning faction “wins the war” in canon.

Every month, automatically

  • Season ends, then faction victory tallies are read from the battle log we already keep.
  • The AI writes the next chapter beat from those results, inside the established lore.
  • An AI-generated story video (~30 to 60s, generated via the same API family that draws our art) renders the chapter: the fall of a bastion, a troll horde breaking through, a warden's last stand.
  • Premiered in the portal and pushed to community Discords: a monthly episode of a show the players themselves scripted by playing.

And the world answers back

The next chapter's card pool responds to the outcome: the victorious faction's tribes surge in the new sealed pool, the defeated faction gets its vengeance arc. Playing the game is voting on the story: every duel you fight is a line in next month's episode, and the season finale premieres a film your battles wrote.

Fairness doctrine applies here too: the faction wins, never a server or a player: the chronicle celebrates a colour, and every player of every server shapes it equally.
